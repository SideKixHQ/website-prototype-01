Free Government Resources for Small Business Owners

A large publicly funded support system exists for small businesses. Most people entitled to use it never do.

Published 27 August 2026 Reading time 4 minutes Category Community

There is a large, publicly funded system in the United States that exists to help people start and run small businesses, and most of the people entitled to use it never do. It is free at the point of use, funded whether or not anyone shows up, and it covers mentoring, training, financing and federal contracting.

This is what it consists of, what each part does, who it is for, and how to reach it.

Why so few people use it

The main reason is that it is not marketed the way commercial services are. Nobody is buying ads to tell you a retired chief executive will mentor you for nothing. The system is discoverable rather than promoted, so you have to know it exists.

The second reason is a suspicion that free help must be low quality. In this case the opposite tends to be true. SBDC advisors and SCORE mentors are frequently people who have run substantial businesses.

SCORE

The largest network of volunteer business mentors in the country. Mentors are current and former business owners who give their time, and the service costs nothing.

What makes SCORE different from a one-off consultation is that it is ongoing. You can keep the same mentor for years, meeting by email, phone or video. They also run workshops, webinars and on-demand courses.

Best for: anyone at any stage who wants a sounding board with real operating experience.

Small Business Development Centers

SBDCs provide free one-to-one advising and low-cost training, usually hosted at universities. Advisors tend to go deeper on financials, business planning and access to capital than general mentoring does.

If you need someone to work through a cash flow projection or pressure-test a loan application, this is the part of the network built for it. In North Carolina, the SBTDC operates offices across the state.

Best for: financial planning, business plans, and preparing to seek financing.

Women's Business Centers

A national network of entrepreneurship centers offering free to low-cost counseling and training, focused on women starting and growing businesses. They also connect people into federal contracting opportunities and access to capital.

Best for: women at any stage, particularly where contracting or capital access is the goal.

Veterans Business Outreach Centers

Thirty-one centers nationally providing training, counseling and referrals to veterans, service members, National Guard and Reserve members, military spouses and family members.

VBOCs also deliver Boots to Business, the entrepreneurship course run through the military transition programme, and a Reboot version off installation for veterans of any era. North Carolina is served by the center at Fayetteville State University.

Best for: anyone with a military connection, at any stage.

Financing programs

The SBA does not usually lend directly. It guarantees part of a loan issued by a bank, which is what makes a lender willing to say yes where they otherwise would not.

7(a) loans

The agency's primary loan program, covering most general business purposes.

Microloans

Smaller amounts issued through community-based intermediary lenders. Often the realistic option when a bank will not yet consider you.

504 loans

Long-term fixed-rate financing for major fixed assets such as buildings and heavy equipment. Not for working capital or inventory.

Lender Match

Describe what you need and the SBA connects you with participating lenders. It is a referral tool, so you still apply with the lender directly.

Federal certifications

If your business is owned by women, veterans, or socially and economically disadvantaged individuals, or sits in a HUBZone, federal certification opens set-aside contracts that other businesses cannot bid on.

Certification takes paperwork but costs nothing to apply for. For businesses that can serve government contracts, it is one of the highest-value things on this list.

What about grants

This is where expectations most need managing. Federal grants for starting an ordinary small business are rare. Most grant programs target research, specific industries, or particular policy goals rather than general business formation.

The SBA publishes what actually exists, and reading it before spending weeks chasing grants tends to save time.

How to find your local office

The SBA's local assistance tool takes a zip code and returns every funded partner near you, filterable by type. That single search covers SCORE, SBDCs, Women's Business Centers and Veterans Business Outreach Centers together.

The scale of what this supports

For context on the population this serves: the US contains 36.2 million small businesses, 99.9% of all US businesses, and they produced roughly nine in ten net new jobs between March 2023 and March 2024. Census data puts nonemployer businesses at 78.4% of all establishments.

How to get the most out of a free advisor

The service is free, which means the constraint is your preparation rather than your budget. A vague first meeting produces vague advice.

Arrive with a specific question rather than a general one. "Should I start a business" is difficult to answer usefully. "Here are my projected fixed costs and my expected price, does this breakeven look realistic" is answerable in a single session.

Bring numbers where numbers exist. An advisor working from actual figures gives you materially better input than one working from description.

And treat it as ongoing. SCORE mentoring in particular is designed to continue, and the value compounds because the mentor accumulates context about your business.

Data resources most people never open

Beyond advising, several federal datasets are genuinely useful for a small business and free to access.

Census Business Formation Statistics

Monthly counts of new business applications, nationally and by state. Useful for seeing whether formation in your area is rising or falling.

BLS Business Employment Dynamics

Openings, closings and survival rates by cohort. This is the actual source behind most survival statistics quoted secondhand, and reading it directly is more informative than reading about it.

SBA state profiles

A profile for every state, updated annually, with business counts, employment share, establishment openings and closings, and lending data.

Census Business Builder

A tool built specifically to help entrepreneurs examine markets and locations, using census data you would otherwise have to assemble yourself.

All of these are public domain, which means you can use the figures in your own business plan or pitch without permission. Our market data page pulls the most relevant numbers together with links to each source.

Training and courses

Alongside one-to-one help, most of the network runs structured training.

SCORE offers workshops, webinars and on-demand courses. SBDCs run low-cost training programs, frequently through their host universities. VBOCs deliver Boots to Business through the military transition program, with a Reboot version available off installation for veterans of any era.

The SBA also runs a virtual summit during National Small Business Week each May, free to attend.

What the system does not do

Worth being clear about the limits so expectations are accurate.

Advisors do not provide legal representation or file your taxes. They will explain what a structure means and point you to what applies, and for the filing itself you need a professional.

They do not lend money directly. The SBA guarantees loans issued by banks rather than issuing most of them itself.

And they do not run your business. Advising is input to your decisions rather than a substitute for making them.

Why this is underused

The size of the gap is worth stating. The US contains 36.2 million small businesses. The number who make contact with a funded advisor in any given year is a small fraction of that.

Part of it is discoverability, part of it is the assumption that free means low quality, and part is that asking for help sits uncomfortably with the way entrepreneurship is usually described.

None of those are good reasons to leave a funded resource unused, particularly one where the alternative is guessing.

Frequently asked questions

What free help is available for small business owners?

SCORE provides free volunteer mentoring. Small Business Development Centers provide free one-to-one advising. Women's Business Centers and Veterans Business Outreach Centers serve specific groups. All are SBA-funded and searchable by zip code.

Is SCORE mentoring really free?

Yes. SCORE mentors are volunteers, mostly current and former business owners, and the service costs nothing. Mentoring is ongoing rather than a single session and can run by email, phone or video.

What is the difference between SCORE and an SBDC?

SCORE offers ongoing mentoring from experienced business people. SBDCs, usually hosted at universities, go deeper on financials, business plans and access to capital. Many people use both.

Are there federal grants for starting a small business?

Rarely. Most federal grant programs target research, specific industries or particular policy goals rather than general business formation. The SBA publishes what actually exists, which is worth reading before spending time chasing grants.

How do I find SBA help near me?

The SBA's local assistance tool takes a zip code and returns every funded partner in your area, filterable by type. One search covers SCORE, SBDCs, Women's Business Centers and Veterans Business Outreach Centers.

What does an SBA loan guarantee mean?

The SBA does not usually lend directly. It guarantees part of a loan issued by a bank, which reduces the lender's risk and makes approval possible where it otherwise would not be.
