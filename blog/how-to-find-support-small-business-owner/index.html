How to Find Support as a Small Business Owner (Even When You Don't Know Where to Look)

Discover how to find support as a small business owner — even when you don't know where to look.

Published 23 May 2026 Reading time 1 minutes Category Community

Support Is Not a Luxury — It's a Strategy

One of the biggest myths in entrepreneurship is that success means doing it alone. The most successful founders actively seek, build, and invest in support systems. Here's how to find yours.

1. Define What Kind of Support You Need

Support comes in many forms: emotional support (someone to talk to), strategic support (a mentor or advisor), operational support (tools or team), and financial support (funding or resources). Knowing which you're lacking helps you find it faster.

2. Find a Mentor in Your Industry

A great mentor can compress years of learning into months. Look for someone 2-5 steps ahead of you — not necessarily a celebrity founder, but someone who's navigated what you're facing now. SCORE, local SBDCs, and platforms like SideKix are great places to start.

3. Join a Mastermind or Peer Group

Masterminds are small, trusted groups of entrepreneurs who meet regularly to share challenges, hold each other accountable, and offer strategic perspective. They're one of the highest-ROI investments an entrepreneur can make — and many are free.

4. Tap Local Resources

Most cities have free or low-cost entrepreneurship resources: Small Business Development Centers (SBDCs), SCORE chapters, local chambers, and economic development programs. These are often underutilized goldmines.

5. Build Your Own Inner Circle

Don't wait for a formal program. Start small — find 2-3 entrepreneurs you respect and propose a monthly check-in. These informal relationships often become your most valuable support system over time.

Frequently asked questions

Where can small business owners find free support?

SCORE (score.org) offers free mentoring from experienced business professionals. SBDCs provide free advising and low-cost training. SideKix connects you with a community of entrepreneurs who support each other every day.

How do I ask for help without feeling weak?

Reframe asking for help as a strategic move, not a weakness. The best entrepreneurs are excellent at leveraging others' knowledge. Asking for help is how you grow faster.
